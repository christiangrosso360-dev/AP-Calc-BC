Why this exists.

I'm a high school student, and I built this while taking AP Calculus BC.

Most of what I could find online was a wall of symbols. The steps were all there, and I could follow them, and I still couldn't picture what was happening. A Riemann sum was a formula before it was ever a shape. So I started making the pictures I wanted — first for myself, then for the whole course.

Everything here is built around that idea: if you can see it move, you understand it in a way that memorising the formula never gets you.

What's actually here

All ten College Board units, with 49 lessons built around interactive visuals rather than static diagrams — you drag things and the maths responds. Every unit has its own exam-practice page and a cheat sheet.

There's a Visualizer that takes a problem you paste in — your homework, not a canned example — draws it, and then walks you through it Socratically instead of handing you the answer.

And there's a full timed mock exam in the real 2027 format. It reads your written free-response answers against the actual rubric, scores you out of 108, predicts a 1–5, and tells you how many questions the clock cost you — which is the part a paper practice test physically cannot do.

A correct answer with no work earns nothing here, because that's how real readers score it in May.

Free, and staying free

No account, no ads, no email wall, nothing to buy. If it's useful, tell someone in your class. That's the whole business model.

It won't be perfect

One person built this, so there will be mistakes in it. If you find a wrong answer, a broken graph or an explanation that doesn't make sense, I genuinely want to know — that's how it gets better.
