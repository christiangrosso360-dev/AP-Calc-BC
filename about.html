Why this exists.

I took AP Calculus BC, and the whole time I was studying for it I could never find a good website to learn from.

It was always review videos. Someone works through their problems on a whiteboard, and you either follow along or you don't — and if the thing you're stuck on isn't the thing they happened to pick, you're on your own. None of it was tailored to me, or to what I was actually struggling with.

The bigger problem was that there was no way to picture the course. Calculus is about things moving and changing, and it kept being handed to me as a page of symbols.

So the summer after I finished the class — before starting mechanical engineering at the University of Miami — I built Unit 10, sequences and series, mostly for fun. Then I looked at it and realized I could build something that didn't exist yet. So I kept going until the whole course was done.

Everything here follows from that

All ten College Board units, 49 lessons, and every one of them built around something you can actually move rather than a diagram you look at. Drag a point and the math responds. That's the part the videos couldn't give me.

The rest of it comes from the other complaint — that nothing was tailored to me. So the two biggest things on the site work on your problem, not somebody else's:

The Visualizer takes a problem you paste in — your homework, the question you're actually stuck on — draws it with your numbers, and then walks you through it instead of just handing over the answer.

And the mock exam reads what you wrote. It marks your free-response against the real rubric point by point, scores you out of 108, predicts a 1–5, and tells you how many questions the clock cost you — which is the one thing a paper practice test physically cannot do.

A correct answer with no work earns nothing here, because that's how real readers score it in May.

Free, and staying free

No account, no ads, no email wall, nothing to buy. If it's useful, tell someone in your class. That's the whole business model.

It won't be perfect

One person built this, so there will be mistakes in it. If you find a wrong answer, a broken graph or an explanation that doesn't make sense, I genuinely want to know — that's how it gets better.

Found something wrong? Email me.

— Christian Grosso
